# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

Plain static HTML/CSS/JS. The current `index.html` is a design-tool export (an `<x-dc>` artboard rendered by `support.js` and vendored React) and is a reference mockup only, not production code; the production site re-implements it as static files.

## Users

1. **Worried professionals (primary).** English-speaking professionals in the US, Europe and Latin America who fear AI will change their profession. Curious and mostly non-technical. They must understand the experiment in seconds and feel "this could be me."
2. **Founders and small business owners under pressure.** They want to know whether this method could work in their own company.
3. **Technical builders.** They come to copy the protocol and the agents.

All three mostly arrive from social media, on mobile, with low attention.

## Product Purpose

Onhundred runs Social Experiment 001: its founder, Ale Sánchez (Subject 001), hands his life to AI in public (his company, his money, and the way he takes care of the people he loves) and logs what is delegated, what breaks and what is left. The site makes that experiment legible and lets people follow and join it.

Success means:
- growing the weekly **Observe** email list (one observation log a week);
- **companies submitting themselves** to the experiment.

## Positioning

A real, named person running his actual company and life through AI agents in public, with an open protocol anyone can copy and a log that includes failures. It is a live experiment with stakes, not a course, a think-piece or a product demo.

## Operating Context

- Visitors land from social posts on a phone, mid-scroll. The page must create curiosity first and show a clear method second.
- The experiment runs in **cycles**, each with three stages: **Map → Delegate → Review**.
- A life is split into three areas: **Relationships, Personal, Company**. Each mapped loop moves through delegation states: **unmapped → mapped → assisted → delegated → autonomous**.
- Individual **experiments** (EXP-01, EXP-02…) are framed as questions and carry a status: **Running, Concluded, Not started, Terminated**.
- The open protocol and agents live at `https://delegate.onhundred.com`.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Observe signup by email (weekly observation log).
- Company submission to the experiment, with a limited number of monthly slots (number undecided).
- Voting on the next experiment (exists on the page; not a success metric).
- Link out to the open protocol.
- Must ship in **English and Spanish from launch**.
- **Consent protocol (binding):** everyone involved knows and can stop it; children are never subjects; no clinical data; "do not try this at home."
- Undecided: cycle/day tracking source, voting mechanics and close dates, submission slot count, email provider, Spanish copy.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: **Onhundred**, "Social Experiment 001."
- Subject 001 is **Ale Sánchez** (`https://alesanchezr.com`), credited with **4Geeks** (`https://4geeks.com`).
- Core lines from the existing copy: "What remains of a person when everything is delegated?" and "Delegate agency. Reclaim your life."
- Manifesto premise in the subject's first-person voice: "AI nearly destroyed my company. Yours could be next. So I'm handing my life to AI… Let's see what's left of me."

## Evidence on Hand

- **Real:** the manifesto and premise copy, the consent protocol, the Map/Delegate/Review method and delegation-state vocabulary.
- **Placeholders, not facts:** every experiment title and status (EXP-01 to EXP-0X), the delegation grid counts, cycle day, voting dates and submission slots.
- No testimonials, press, subscriber counts, results or company case studies exist yet. Future work must not fabricate any of them.

## Product Principles

1. **Curiosity first, method second.** Hook in the first seconds on mobile; explain how it works only after the visitor cares.
2. **"This could be me."** Frame everything so a non-technical professional sees their own job and life in it.
3. **Open by default.** The protocol and agents are public and copyable, and failures are logged as visibly as successes.
4. **Consent is non-negotiable.** No experiment, story or data point bypasses the consent protocol.
5. **Only real data.** Show experiments, counts and outcomes only when they actually happened; label placeholders as such.
